# Content guidelines

These apply to every article, explanation, editorial cross-reference and UI text. Nothing is
published under them yet (2026-10-11). They are the rules for Phase 3 onward.

## Truthfulness
1. Never invent quotations, references, manuscript details or historical claims. A quotation that
   cannot be traced to an identifiable edition and locator is not presented as a quotation.
2. Cite patristic sources precisely: author, work, section, edition or translator, then paragraph
   or page where available, e.g. *Athanasius, On the Incarnation 54 (NPNF² 4, tr. Robertson)*.
3. Keep apart what the evidence shows, what the Church teaches, what is common interpretation, and
   what is scholarly hypothesis or personal opinion. Say which one each claim is.
4. Where scholars disagree, say so. Don't overstate historical arguments. For example, evidence
   that early Christians existed is not evidence for a specific doctrine.
5. State objections in their strongest fair form before answering them. Don't caricature skeptics
   or other traditions.

## Tradition
6. Explain Coptic Orthodox teaching on its own terms. Don't silently substitute Protestant, Roman
   Catholic or Eastern Orthodox assumptions.
7. Represent other traditions accurately, and name them.
8. For Christology, distinguish Miaphysite teaching from Eutychian Monophysitism, using reviewed sources.

## Provenance and status
9. Every source an article uses must be in the source registry.
10. AI-assisted or AI-generated text must set `review.origin` accordingly, and it is labeled.
11. Nothing is presented as approved by the Coptic Orthodox Church without explicit written authorization.

## Language
12. Arabic and English versions share one `id`. A translation is reviewed in its own right.
13. Plain words, sentence case and short paragraphs. Write for a thoughtful reader who is not a specialist.
