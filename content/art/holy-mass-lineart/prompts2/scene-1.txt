Scene: The haikal screen (iconostasis) of a Coptic Orthodox church seen from the nave, drawn accurately. It is a solid wooden screen of dark ebony-brown wood decorated with ivory and mother-of-pearl geometric inlay (crosses and eight-pointed star patterns) and carved crosses on every post and panel. It has THREE doors: a large central Royal Door in an arch that rises high, with a heavy embroidered crimson curtain tied back on both sides with tassels, and two smaller side doors with their own curtains; above each side door a small window. To the viewer's right of the Royal Door the icon of Christ enthroned (blessing, holding a Gospel), to the viewer's left of it the icon of the Virgin Mary with the Child; further icons of an archangel and the Baptism. Directly above the Royal Door the Last Supper (Christ in the centre, twelve apostles with halos around a long table), and at the very top the Crucifixion with the Virgin and St John, crowned with crosses. A hanging oil lamp burns before each icon (except Christ's) and ostrich eggs hang on chains between the icons. The priest in a white tunic and crimson-and-gold cross vestment, seen from behind, enters through the right side of the Royal Door barefoot, having left his sandals at the step; two deacons in white tunics with crossing stoles stand on the raised three-step khorus platform in front beside two tall candlesticks with lit candles. Stone floor tiles in perspective.
